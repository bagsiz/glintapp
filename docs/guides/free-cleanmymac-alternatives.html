Free CleanMyMac alternatives for macOS

Last updated 13 November 2026

CleanMyMac from MacPaw is one of the best‑known Mac utilities: it bundles cleanup, an uninstaller, maintenance and malware checks in one polished app, and it's paid after a trial. If you'd rather not pay, the same jobs can be covered by free tools, most of them small and focused. Here's what each one is good at, so you can pick only what you need.

What a "cleaner" actually does

Before choosing, it helps to split the job into parts. All‑in‑one apps combine these, but you may only need one or two:

  1. See where the space went: disk maps and large‑file lists.
  2. Remove junk: caches, logs, developer leftovers, old installers.
  3. Uninstall apps with their leftover files.
  4. Maintenance: reindex Spotlight, flush DNS, rebuild caches.
  5. Privacy and security: browser data, login items, suspicious background services.

Seeing where your space went

Start here, because the biggest wins are usually a few large items, not thousands of cache files. OmniDiskSweeper is the quickest way to drill into folders by size. GrandPerspective draws each file as a rectangle sized by how much space it takes, so one huge video or VM image jumps out. Both need Full Disk Access to see everything. Without a tool, you can do the same in Terminal; see How to find large files on a Mac.

Uninstalling apps properly

Dragging an app to the Trash leaves its support files behind. AppCleaner and Pearcleaner both find those files by the app's bundle ID and let you review the list before deleting. Pearcleaner can also scan for leftovers of apps you removed in the past. The manual method is in How to completely uninstall apps on a Mac.

Maintenance

OnyX has been around for many macOS versions and exposes a lot of system options: cache cleaning, rebuilding the Launch Services database, Spotlight reindexing, and hidden Finder and Dock settings. It's powerful, so read each option before running it. Most maintenance jobs also have a one‑line Terminal equivalent, for example sudo mdutil -E / to reindex Spotlight, or sudo dscacheutil -flushcache; sudo killall -HUP mDNSResponder to flush DNS.

Security and background items

macOS has its own malware protection (XProtect and Gatekeeper). For a look at what runs in the background, KnockKnock is a well‑regarded free tool from a long‑time Mac security researcher. System Settings › General › Login Items also shows most background items; see How to remove login items and launch agents.

How to choose a cleaner, any cleaner

  • It shows you everything before deleting. A list with sizes, not a single "Clean" button with a number.
  • You can undo. Deleted items go to the Trash or a quarantine, at least for anything that isn't a cache.
  • It stays away from system files. Nothing under /System, and no "registry"‑style cleaning; macOS doesn't have a registry.
  • It's signed and notarized, so macOS opens it without warnings, and you can tell who made it.
  • It doesn't scare you. Warnings of "hundreds of problems" on a healthy Mac are a sales tactic.
  • It respects your privacy. Check whether the app uploads file names or scan results anywhere.

FAQ

Do I need a cleaner app on a Mac at all?

No. macOS manages caches and temporary files on its own, and everything a cleaner does can be done by hand with Finder and Terminal. Cleaner apps save time and show you where space went; they're a convenience, not a requirement.

Are free Mac cleaners safe?

The good ones are. Look for a signed and notarized app from a developer you can identify, a clear list of what will be deleted before anything is removed, and a way to undo. Be wary of any app that warns of “dozens of problems” to sell an upgrade.

Is CleanMyMac bad?

No. CleanMyMac is a polished, long-running commercial app from MacPaw. The tools here are for people who'd rather not pay or prefer smaller, single-purpose apps.
